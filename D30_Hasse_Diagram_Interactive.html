1. What is D₃₀?

Let D₃₀ be the set of positive divisors of 30, ordered by divisibility.

Elements

{1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15, 30}

Order rule

a ≤ b ⇔ a | b

So “lower” means “divides”.

How do we find them?

Since 30 = 2 × 3 × 5, every divisor chooses whether to contain each prime factor:

1235 6 = 2×310 = 2×515 = 3×530 = 2×3×5

2. Comparable vs. non-comparable

Two elements are comparable if one divides the other. Otherwise they are non-comparable (incomparable).

✅ Comparable

2 | 6, so 2 and 6 are comparable.

5 | 30, so 5 and 30 are comparable.

❌ Non-comparable

2 ∤ 3 and 3 ∤ 2.

Therefore 2 and 3 are incomparable.

Important incomparable pairs in D₃₀

2 ↔ 32 ↔ 53 ↔ 5 6 ↔ 106 ↔ 1510 ↔ 15

There are other incomparable pairs too, such as 3 with 10 and 5 with 6. The key test is always: “Does either number divide the other?”

3. Why do incomparable elements appear on different-looking branches?

A Hasse diagram does not place elements according to their numerical size. It places them according to the partial order.

Example: 6 and 10

6 = 2×3 and 10 = 2×5.

Neither divides the other, so there is no order relation between them. They therefore occupy separate branches at the same level.

Level idea

Level 0: elements with nothing below them → 1

Level 1: 2, 3, 5

Level 2: 6, 10, 15

Level 3: 30

Why same level?

6, 10 and 15 each contain exactly two of the three prime factors 2, 3, 5. None divides another.

So they naturally form the same “layer” in the standard graded drawing.

4. Remove transitive connections

This is the trick that makes a Hasse diagram clean.

Ordinary divisibility graph

It could show every relation, including 1|6, 1|30, and 2|30.

That becomes cluttered.

Hasse rule

Keep only cover relations: connect a to b when a|b and there is no divisor strictly between them.

Cover relations for D₃₀

1–21–31–5 2–62–10 3–63–15 5–105–15 6–3010–3015–30

Notice: there is no direct line from 1 to 6, because 1 → 2 → 6 or 1 → 3 → 6 already tells us 1 divides 6.

5. Watch the Hasse diagram being constructed

Instead of showing the finished diagram immediately, watch it grow from the bottom upward. Each new element appears only after the relations needed to place it are established.

Start: place 1 at the bottom because 1 divides every element of D₃₀.

Why this movement?

The construction follows the partial order: smaller divisors are placed below their multiples.

Watch for this

When 2, 3 and 5 appear, they do not get joined to one another because they are incomparable.
